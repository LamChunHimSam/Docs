01 — Context

Why Unpriced Securities Exist

Traditional VC financing requires agreeing on a price (valuation) upfront. As the cost of starting companies fell and more non-institutional investors entered the ecosystem, pricing early-stage startups became difficult, slow, and contentious.

Unpriced securities solve this by deferring pricing to the next round. The investor gives money now; the contract specifies the rules for determining how much equity they'll receive later.

Key idea
The angel doesn't receive shares at the time of investment. They receive a right to convert into shares at the next priced round, at a price determined by the round — subject to a discount or cap.

Timeline

Seed
Angel invests via SAFE. No shares issued. No price set.
Between
Startup operates. SAFE sits as a contract, not equity.
Series A
VC sets price. SAFE converts. Angel receives shares.
Exit / IPO
Preferred converts to common. Payoffs realised.

The cap table at the angel stage has only one entry: the founder (100%). Angels hold a contract. The full cap table crystallises only at Series A when the SAFE converts.

02 — Mechanics

How a SAFE Works

A SAFE specifies three key terms: the purchase amount, a valuation cap, and a discount rate. When a qualifying financing occurs (e.g. Series A), the angel converts at whichever price gives them more shares.

The Two Conversion Prices

// SAFE Price — based on the valuation cap SAFE Price = Valuation Cap ÷ Company Capitalisation (shares existing BEFORE investment, excl. SAFEs) // Discount Price — based on the Series A round price Discount Price = Series A Share Price × Discount Rate e.g. $X × 0.80 (for an 80% discount rate) // Angel uses whichever gives MORE shares = lower price Conversion Price = min(SAFE Price, Discount Price) Angel Shares = Purchase Amount ÷ Conversion Price
Note on discount rate
An 80% discount rate means the angel pays 80% of the Series A price — i.e. a 20% discount to the round. Lower conversion price = more shares for the angel.

The Circularity Problem

This is what makes SAFEs tricky to calculate. The discount price depends on the Series A share price X. But X depends on how many total shares exist — which depends on how many shares the angel gets — which depends on X.

This is a fixed-point problem solved with simultaneous equations. The key insight from class:

"The price of the round is itself dependent on whether [the angel] chooses the cap or the discount. That's the circularity — I choose the discount price versus the cap based on the price, but the price depends on whether I choose the cap or the discount."

Solving the Circularity

Use the post-money valuation constraint. If post-money = $V$, share price = $x$, then total shares = $V/x$. Write all share counts in terms of $x$, set them equal to $V/x$, and solve.

Founder shares + Angel shares + VC shares = Total shares // Substituting in terms of x (assuming discount binds): 1,250,000 + (800,000 / 0.8x) + (3,000,000 / x) = 10,000,000 / x // Rearrange: 1,250,000 = (10M - 1M - 3M) / x = 6,000,000 / x x = $4.80
03 — Instruments

Convertible Note vs SAFE vs Preferred Stock

Feature Convertible Note SAFE Conv. Preferred Stock
Security type Debt Equity contract Equity (preferred)
Converts into Preferred stock Preferred stock Common stock
When converts Next financing or maturity Next qualifying financing IPO / liquidity event
Maturity date Yes — creates repayment risk No N/A
Interest Yes No N/A
Speed / simplicity Fast, less diligence Fastest, simplest Slower — full negotiation
Price set at Deferred to next round Deferred to next round Time of investment
Practical takeaway
For most sophisticated early-stage deals today, a SAFE is preferred over a convertible note — no maturity risk, simpler contract, no interest accrual. However, some angels still prefer convertible notes for their debt-like protections.
04 — In-Class Assignment

Working Through the SAFE Cap Table

Given Angels invest $800k via SAFE · Valuation cap $5M · Discount rate 80% · Founder has 1,250,000 shares · Series A raises $3M at $10M post-money valuation

Step-by-Step Method

  1. Calculate the SAFE Price
    SAFE Price = Valuation Cap ÷ Company Capitalisation (founder shares only, excluding SAFEs)
    SAFE Price = $5,000,000 ÷ 1,250,000 = $4.00 / share
    This is fixed regardless of scenario.
  2. Set up the equation for share price X
    Let X = Series A price per share. Use the post-money valuation constraint: total shares = $10M / X.
    Founder + Angel + VC = 10,000,000 / X 1,250,000 + (Angel investment / conversion price) + (3,000,000 / X) = 10,000,000 / X
  3. Assume discount binds, solve for X
    If discount binds, angel conversion price = 0.8X. Substitute and solve:
    1,250,000 + (800,000 / 0.8X) + (3,000,000 / X) = 10,000,000 / X 1,250,000 + (1,000,000 / X) + (3,000,000 / X) = 10,000,000 / X 1,250,000 = 6,000,000 / X X = $4.80
  4. Verify which mechanism binds
    Compare SAFE price ($4.00) vs Discount price (0.8 × $4.80 = $3.84). Angel picks whichever is lower (gives more shares).
    Discount price = 0.8 × $4.80 = $3.84 SAFE price = $4.00 → $3.84 < $4.00 → Discount binds ✓ (assumption in step 3 was correct)
  5. Calculate all share counts and build cap table
    Angel shares = $800,000 / $3.84 = 208,333 VC shares = $3,000,000 / $4.80 = 625,000 Founder = 1,250,000 Total = 2,083,333 Check: $10M / 2,083,333 = $4.80 ✓

05 — Intuition

Why the Cap Matters for Incentives

Beyond the maths, the cap has an important economic function: it aligns incentives between the angel and the founder.

Without a cap
The angel converts at a fixed 20% discount to whatever the Series A price is. If the company's valuation goes up a lot, the Series A price is high — meaning the angel pays more per share and gets fewer shares. The angel's return from a higher valuation is diluted away. This creates a perverse incentive: the angel may not want to help push the valuation up.
With a cap
Once the Series A price exceeds the cap-implied price, the angel's shares are fixed. They are now fully "inside" alongside the founder. Both parties benefit equally from a higher valuation — their incentives are perfectly aligned. As Prof. Nanda put it: "Give them a low cap, make sure that they're fully incentivised, and then you both go and raise at a very high valuation."

Share Price Intuition — Scenario B

When the cap binds in Scenario B, angels convert at $4.00 instead of $10.24 (discount price). This means angels take more shares. More angel shares means the VC needs more shares to reach their target 15%. More total shares at the same $3M investment means the share price must be lower — which is why it comes in at $11.73, not $12.80.

This is the core circularity: angel's choice affects VC's price, which affects angel's choice.
